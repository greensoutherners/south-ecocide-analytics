The investigation, step by step.

1. A river, and a line

The Litani rises in the Bekaa, runs south between the mountains, cuts its gorge below Beaufort Castle and turns west to reach the sea north of Tyre. 69.8 km of its mapped course lie in the South and Nabatieh governorates.

Under UN Security Council resolution 1701 of 2006, the ground between the Blue Line and the river is to hold no armed personnel or weapons other than those of the Lebanese state and UNIFIL. The river gives that ground its name: south of the Litani.

2. The places along its banks

30 places of the gazetteer lie within 2 km of the river in the south. The corpus holds 52 fires, 37 white phosphorus attacks and 64 displacement orders for them, each kind counted apart.

3. Fire along the river

52 fires linked to an Israeli attack burned at these places, from 17 March 2026 to 4 October 2026. The most at Zawtar El Sharqiyeh (13), Arnoun (9) and Yohmor (7).

Where a burn scar was measured, 30 of them cover 614.2 ha between them.

4. The gorge below Beaufort

Where the river turns west it runs in a gorge hundreds of metres deep, under Beaufort Castle, with Zawtar El Sharqiyeh and Yohmor above its banks.

The Village Studio mapped both: in Yohmor 245 buildings destroyed; in Zawtar El Sharqiyeh 262 buildings changed on Sentinel-2.

5. South of the river

Across the whole corpus, 353 of the fires from attacks burned at places the gazetteer counts south of the Litani, and 85 at places north of it. 13 burned at places it does not put on either side.

The fires are drawn here on both banks, so the river can be read as what it is on this map: a line the war's fire crossed.

Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • Everything here is the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. The wildlife and trees map, where it is shown, is earlier context recorded in 2023 and 2024, and says so.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
