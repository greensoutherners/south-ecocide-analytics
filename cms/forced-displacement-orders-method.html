Orders to leave issued to the towns of South Lebanon in the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026, recorded from the issuing party's own published statements, one record per order and place. This page sets out what an order record holds, how the towns it names are drawn, and what the record does not establish.

What a record is

A record is one order to one place: who was told to leave, when, by what means, and whether an attack followed. The primary source is the issuing party's own published statement, kept as it was published.

The most orders name Zrariye (11), Habboush (11), Arabsalim (11), Deir ez-Zahrani (10), Harouf (9) and Houmine el Faouqa (9). The busiest month was May 2026, with 267 orders.

Orders to whole regions

35 orders address a whole region rather than a town. The database draws each region once, as a violet area with a dashed edge, named with its orders, and shows it while one of its orders is in the dates.

Orders to everyone south of the Zahrani river came 12 times between March 2026 and June 2026, 3 of them addressed to all of South Lebanon with the Zahrani as the line. The ground south of the Zahrani is about 1,703 km², 84% of the South and Nabatieh governorates. Everyone south of the Litani was told to leave once, in March 2026: about 1,204 km², 60% of the South and Nabatieh governorates. Beirut's southern suburbs were named in 16 orders between March 2026 and April 2026. Tyre, its camps and the neighbourhoods round it were named in 5 orders between March 2026 and June 2026. In April 2026 every vessel at sea between Tyre and Ras al-Naqoura was told to sail north of Tyre.

The regions are drawn from the orders' words, because the maps the orders came with are not held. South of a river is the South and Nabatieh governorates on the far side of the river from the sea, the river as OpenStreetMap maps it; where the Litani runs north up the Marjayoun valley, its east bank is on the far side. Tyre with its camps, and the southern suburbs, are the municipal areas that hold the places the orders name. The sea is Lebanon's territorial sea, 12 nautical miles out, between the two places the order names. An order that names a region is counted on it, and on a town only where it names the town too.

What followed the orders

For every order that names a town, the corpus looks for an attack at that town from the moment the order was posted to the end of the next day: a strike reported in the archive of the news channels the corpus follows, a Public Data attack event inside the town's land, or a record of its own (a detonation, a strike, white phosphorus). A report of the order itself, a resistance claim, and a mention of the district or of a town of the same name are not counted.

475 orders were followed by an attack at their town in that time. For 189, no attack was found, with every source covering those days. 37 cannot be assessed: most name no town, and one comes after the Public Data feed ends.

Limits
  • An order records what was announced, not how many people left.
  • Followed by an attack is a coincidence of town and time: an attack documented at the town after the order, never a finding that the order and the attack were one operation.
  • No attack found is a checked negative in these sources, not evidence that none happened: the corpus does not collect every attack. For most orders the hour is known, but a record or a Public Data event gives only a day, so an attack on the order's own day from those sources may have come before it.
  • A region is drawn from the order's words, because the maps the orders came with are not held. It shows where an order told people to leave, not what happened there.
Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • Everything here is the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. The wildlife and trees map, where it is shown, is earlier context recorded in 2023 and 2024, and says so.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
