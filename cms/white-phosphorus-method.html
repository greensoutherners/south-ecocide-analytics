Geolocated and not geolocated

An attack is geolocated when it was placed on the ground: matched to the place in its footage, to a satellite detection of the fire it lit, or to the burn scar it left. 35 attacks are geolocated.

An attack is not geolocated when its village is known and its spot is not. 93 attacks are not geolocated. Not geolocated does not mean the attack is in doubt: it means the reports name the village and nothing has yet placed the shells within it. On the map both are counted at their town.

How an attack is recorded

A record is one attack: the white phosphorus reported over one place on one day. It is built from the reports that name it, the footage of it where any was published, and the satellite detections of the fires it lit. Shell counts are kept where the sources give them: 147 shells across 102 records, as the sources state them, not a munitions inventory.

White phosphorus burns what it lands on. Where an attack lit a fire, the fire is its own record in the fires corpus, filed as a fire lit by white phosphorus, so the burned ground can be read beside the attack.

Limits
  • An attack that is not geolocated places its village, not its shells.
  • Shell counts are what the sources state.
  • Where footage exists, the munition is identified from what it shows; where it does not, from what the reports of the day say.
Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • Everything here is the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. The wildlife and trees map, where it is shown, is earlier context recorded in 2023 and 2024, and says so.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
