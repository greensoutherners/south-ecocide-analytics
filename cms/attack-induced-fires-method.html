Which fires are shown

The yellow-line zone is the ground along the border that the Israeli army declared or held; 364 of the fires burned inside it, with or without an attack linked. Beaufort Castle and the towns round it are the subject of one of the investigations, so every fire there is shown: the 49 fires recorded there since 1 March 2026 all lie in the yellow-line zone as well, so that rule adds none of its own today.

What a record is

A record is one fire at one place in one period: not one satellite detection and not one report. Thermal detections are clustered into a fire before a record is made, and records shown to describe the same fire are folded together. An Israeli military attack is linked to a fire when an attack is documented at the same town on a Beirut day of the fire, in the corpus's own records of detonations, strikes and phosphorus, in public attack data for the town's territory or in a media report of the attack, or when a source on the fire itself names the attack.

218 fires carry an exact location, from a satellite detection cluster or a burnt area outline, and 233 are known by their village and drawn at it.

What lit them

The link to an attack also says what kind of attack it was. Where white phosphorus is documented the fire is filed as white phosphorus; an incendiary munition as incendiary; illumination flares as flares; and any other documented attack as a fire lit by an explosion. The 107 fires with no attack linked are filed as unidentified: the corpus holds no evidence of what lit them, which is not evidence that nothing did.

Where they burned

The most fires are recorded at Al-Mansouri (21), Meiss ej Jabal (18), Haddatha (16), Al-Khiyam (15) and Kounine (15). 20 fires burned on tree-covered ground, 13 inside a Key Biodiversity Area and 7 inside a protected area, where those tests could be made.

Burned area

The 6,621.7 hectares are the sum of measured burn scars, not an estimate of all the ground that burned: they come from 238 of the 451 fires. A fire without a measured scar adds nothing, so the figure is a floor. Scars are measured on Sentinel-2 passes either side of the fire, over the village's approximate territory, reviewed, and only then added to the record.

Limits of the measurement
  • A fire less than about eight days old cannot be measured: the scar needs a clear pass after it.
  • Freshly turned soil reads dark on satellite imagery in much the same way as char, so a darkening alone is not taken for a burn.

What these figures do not establish

Limits
  • The link to an attack is a documented attack at the same town on the same day. It is the evidence the corpus holds for what lit a fire, recorded as such.
  • Unidentified means no attack is documented at the town on the fire's days. It is not a finding that the fire was not lit by an attack.
  • Hectares are measured for part of the fires only. They are a floor.
  • Reporting is uneven in time, so the record of some months is fuller than others.
Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • Everything here is the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. The wildlife and trees map, where it is shown, is earlier context recorded in 2023 and 2024, and says so.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
