Al-Mansouri lies on the coast south of Tyre, inside the zone the Israeli army declared in the 2026 war. On its sharp satellite pictures readers found 806 buildings destroyed and 125 damaged, most of them first seen gone on the pictures of 19 August 2026 and 8 October 2026, in the weeks when Lebanese media reported the Israeli army detonating houses there week after week (48 reports). This investigation reads the town on those pictures, with its orchards, fields and roads, its fires, white phosphorus and displacement orders.

The detonations, as they were reported

From late July to early October 2026, photographers, television crews and residents filmed the detonations in Al-Mansouri, mostly from the neighbouring village of Hanniyeh on the ridge to the north-east and from the shore of Tyre across the bay. A selection follows, each shown through its publisher's own page or player and credited to it. Green Southerners copied none of these pictures; what each shows is described in our words.

  • Annahar, 11 September 2026: Twenty-five detonations in five hours. Annahar's photographer Ahmad Mantash counted twenty-five detonations in five hours; the report's photographs and video show columns of grey and white dust rolling across the slope, and houses already flattened below them.
  • Associated Press, via KSAT, 11 September 2026: Residents watch drones drop explosives on their village. Associated Press photographs by Hussein Malla, taken from Hanniyeh: residents filming the smoke over Al-Mansouri, and a drone carrying a box on a long line above a damaged house, which the agency's caption describes as explosives to be dropped on houses for detonation.
  • Al Jazeera English, 4 September 2026: A drone releases packages over the slope. A short video: a drone over the wooded ridge lets go of two packages, then explosions throw white and grey smoke up among the houses.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 9 September 2026: The first moments after a large detonation. Al-Araby TV's correspondent Edmond Sassine films the village minutes after a blast: a multi-storey house with collapsed floors, and dust still rising from a building that has just come down.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 13 September 2026: Four detonations at once, filmed across the sea. Filmed from the shore across the bay: four or more white plumes rise at the same moment from the coastal plain at the foot of the hills.
  • L'Orient Today, 9 September 2026: Buildings pulverised. An AFP photograph by Kawnat Haju: a huge tan cloud of dust rolls across the lower town, with gutted houses at its edge.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 28 September 2026: A wall of dust across the slope. Long-lens footage of many blasts at the same moment, sending a wall of grey and beige dust across a slope of ruined buildings under the wooded ridge. Reports that evening said the blast was heard in Tyre, nine kilometres away.
  • Asharq Al-Awsat English, 28 September 2026: Smoke over the town, seen from Hanniyeh. A Reuters photograph: a long cloud of dust rolls along the slope after explosions; most of the houses below it are already destroyed.
  • Courtney Bonneau, and franfran2424's geolocation, on X, 1 October 2026: A demolition filmed, then placed on the map by others. A video by the photographer Courtney Bonneau shows a sequence of detonations on the upper slope of the village. The open-source researcher franfran2424 placed the two houses it shows about 450 m south of the village centre, by matching the slope, the ridge and the gullies.
  • Maariv, 28 September 2026: The army's own account. The Israeli army published photographs of its reservists in the village and said they had destroyed more than six hundred sites there that it called terror infrastructure, among them a tunnel shaft under a house. This is the army's statement, reported as it was published.

How to read it
  • Every building, road and patch of land drawn was read on two pictures by independent readers and counted only when a majority agreed. A change says where and between which pictures, never why or by whom.
  • Buildings on no map of the town from before the war were found on the pictures by readers, and more readers confirmed that each stood before and was hit after.
  • Detonations are counted from the reports found by week. Their collection is not complete, so the count is of reports, not of every detonation. The bulldozing is shown on this town only.
  • The outlines of the orchards span both wars, from August 2023 to October 2026; they say what was lost between the two pictures, not when. Records and outlines are read side by side and never added together.
Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • Everything here is the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. The wildlife and trees map, where it is shown, is earlier context recorded in 2023 and 2024, and says so.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
