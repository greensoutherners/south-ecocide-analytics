Al-Mansouri lies on the coast south of Tyre, inside the zone the Israeli army declared in the 2026 war. On sharp satellite pictures of the town and the ground within 1.5 km of it, taken in 2023, June 2024 and 2026, readers found 1,110 buildings destroyed and 151 damaged. 180 were already destroyed or damaged by the first picture of 2026, taken on 23 May 2026, some of them before the 2026 war; 335 cannot be dated more closely than the pair of pictures that shows them; and 578 were first seen destroyed or damaged on the pictures of 19 August 2026 and 8 October 2026, in the weeks when Lebanese media reported the Israeli army detonating houses there week after week, on 48 days in all. This investigation reads the town on those pictures, with its orchards, fields and roads, its fires, white phosphorus and displacement orders.

The detonations, as they were reported

From late July to early October 2026, photographers, television crews and residents filmed the detonations in Al-Mansouri, mostly from the neighbouring village of Hanniyeh on the ridge to the north-east and from the shore of Tyre across the bay. A selection follows, each shown through its publisher's own page or player and credited to it. Green Southerners copied none of these pictures; what each shows is described in our words. Where a picture's place was checked on the dated satellite pictures, two separate checks had to agree before it is said here.

  • Al Jazeera English, 4 September 2026: A drone releases packages over the slope. A short video: a drone over the wooded ridge lets go of two packages, then explosions throw white and grey smoke up among the houses. Placed by two separate checks among houses that also appear in the Associated Press photographs of 11 September, taken from Hanniyeh. They stand on the pictures of 10 and 19 August 2026, and most of them are rubble on 8 October; the pictures cannot date this blast more closely than that.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 5 September 2026: A white column at the foot of the slope, seen from Tyre. Al-Araby TV's clip opens on older footage; its last shot is a long-lens view across the bay from the southern seafront of Tyre, with a large white column of dust rising at the foot of the village slope. That last shot is placed by two separate checks: the column rises at the foot of the town beside a compound whose buildings stand on the picture of 19 August 2026 and are rubble on 8 October.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 9 September 2026: The first moments after a large detonation. Al-Araby TV's correspondent Edmond Sassine films the village minutes after a blast: a multi-storey house with collapsed floors, and dust still rising from a building that has just come down. Placed by two separate checks: the camera stood on the northern rim at the western edge of Hanniyeh, looking south-south-west at Al-Mansouri.
  • L'Orient Today, 9 September 2026: Buildings pulverised. An AFP photograph by Kawnat Haju: a huge tan cloud of dust rolls across the lower town, with gutted houses at its edge.
  • Annahar, 11 September 2026: Twenty-five detonations in five hours. Annahar's photographer Ahmad Mantash counted twenty-five detonations in five hours; the report's photographs and video show columns of grey and white dust rolling across the slope, and houses already flattened below them. Placed by two separate checks: the camera stood beside the main road in Hanniyeh, looking south-west across to Al-Mansouri. The column in the key photograph rises from among the houses in the middle of the town, which stand on the pictures of 23 May and 10 August 2026, are hidden by smoke on 19 August and are rubble on 8 October.
  • Associated Press, via KSAT, 11 September 2026: Residents watch drones drop explosives on their village. Associated Press photographs by Hussein Malla, taken from Hanniyeh: residents filming the smoke over Al-Mansouri, and a drone carrying a box on a long line above a damaged house, which the agency's caption describes as explosives to be dropped on houses for detonation. Placed by two separate checks, from the photographs' own capture times (11:22 to 14:20 that day) and the far ridge: the camera was in western Hanniyeh, looking south-south-west. The large house under the drone stands on every picture from 2023 to 8 October 2026, damaged as it is in the photograph: it was not levelled in those weeks.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 13 September 2026: Four detonations at once, filmed across the sea. Filmed from the shore across the bay: four or more white plumes rise at the same moment from the coastal plain at the foot of the hills.
  • Annahar, 19 September 2026: The water tower down. Photographs by Ahmad Mantash, taken from Hanniyeh and set beside the view he had less than two weeks earlier: excavators by a long wall, gutted multi-storey frames, and on the hilltop a broken round tank. Placed by two separate checks: the tank is the town's water tower, which stands on the pictures of 10 and 19 August 2026 and lies as a ring on the ground on 8 October, so it fell between 19 August and the day the photographs were published.
  • Al-Araby TV on Telegram, 28 September 2026: A wall of dust across the slope. Long-lens footage of many blasts at the same moment, sending a wall of grey and beige dust across a slope of ruined buildings under the wooded ridge. Reports that evening said the blast was heard in Tyre, nine kilometres away. Placed by two separate checks, one of them against an AFP photograph of the same scene taken that day from the same spot: the row of blasts is in the south-west of the town, along and just north of its east-west road, where the houses stand on every picture up to 19 August 2026 and are rubble or cleared on 8 October.
  • Asharq Al-Awsat English, 28 September 2026: Smoke over the town, seen from Hanniyeh. A Reuters photograph: a long cloud of dust rolls along the slope after explosions; most of the houses below it are already destroyed.
  • Maariv, 28 September 2026: The army's own account. The Israeli army published photographs of its reservists in the village and said they had destroyed more than six hundred sites there that it called terror infrastructure, among them a tunnel shaft under a house. This is the army's statement, reported as it was published.
  • Courtney Bonneau, and franfran2424's geolocation, on X, 1 October 2026: A demolition filmed, then placed on the map by others. A video by the photographer Courtney Bonneau shows a sequence of detonations on the upper slope of the village. The open-source researcher franfran2424 placed the two houses it shows about 450 m south of the village centre, by matching the slope, the ridge and the gullies. Two separate checks of our own confirm the place, and the pictures agree with the day the video was posted: the camera stood on the edge of Hanniyeh, and the first blasts are on three houses on the upper slope south of the centre, which stand on every picture up to 19 August 2026 and are rubble on 8 October, beside a road bulldozed by then.

How to read it
  • Every building and road drawn, and the bulldozed ground, was read on two pictures by independent readers and counted only when a majority agreed; the orchard, field and tree outlines were drawn by hand by Green Southerners. A change says where and between which pictures, never why or by whom.
  • Buildings on no map of the town from before the war were found on the pictures by readers, and more readers confirmed that each stood before and was hit after.
  • Detonations are counted by the days on which they were reported, week by week. The count is of the days found, not of every detonation, and it can rise as more are found.
  • The outlines of the orchards span both wars, from August 2023 to October 2026; they say what was lost between the two pictures, not when. Records and outlines are read side by side and never added together.
Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • The records are those of the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. Pictures and maps from before that day, among them the land cover of 2025 and the map of the wildlife and trees recorded in 2023 and 2024, are earlier context, and each says when it is from.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
Citing this page

Counts rise as more material is collected and can fall when duplicate records are folded together, so a citation needs the date of the snapshot it was read from.
