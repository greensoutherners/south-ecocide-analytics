South Lebanon runs from the sea to the slopes of Mount Hermon: sandy beaches where sea turtles nest, a coastal plain of orchards and fields, the limestone hills of Jabal Amel under oak, terebinth and carob, olive groves and terraces, the gorge of the Litani and the valleys that feed it, and the Marjayoun plain. This page sets that land and what lives on it beside every attack of the 2026 war recorded over it from 1 March 2026: the fires and the white phosphorus.

The land between the sea and Hermon

The South and Nabatieh governorates cover about 2,024 km². On the land cover map of 2025, the year before the war, 43.9% of them is rangeland, the class that takes in the grass, scrub and maquis of the hills; 40.6% built area; 9.2% trees; and 5.9% crops. Water and bare ground share the rest. At 10 m a square that holds part of a house and part of a garden can be read as built, so the built area takes in the gardens, plots and lanes among the houses of the villages as well as the houses.

Most of the south's woodland is scrub rather than forest: evergreen oak, terebinth, carob and thorny broom over the limestone, with olive groves, orchards, tobacco and cereals on the terraces and in the plains, and stands of pine. The wildlife lives across that mosaic: jackals, foxes, wild boar, badgers, hedgehogs and porcupines, the striped hyena and the rock hyrax, the birds of prey that hunt over it and the storks that cross it on migration.

The coast

The southern coast holds some of the longest sandy beaches left in Lebanon. Loggerhead and green turtles nest on them, at the Tyre Coast Nature Reserve south of the old city and on the beach of Al-Mansouri. Behind the beaches the coastal plain is orchards, banana and citrus groves and fields as far as the cliffs of Ras al-Bayada and An-Naqoura. On 20 June 2026 The Guardian and UPI reported that Mona Khalil, a sea turtle conservationist, was killed by an Israeli attack (here and here on this website).

The hills, the river and the plain

Inland the hills rise in terraces to the ridges of the border. The Litani crosses them from the Bekaa in a gorge below Beaufort Castle before turning west to the sea north of Tyre; in the east the Hasbani, fed by the Wazzani springs, runs south from the foot of Hermon. Valleys such as Wadi al-Hujeir, Wadi Saluki and Wadi Zibqin cut through the hills, wooded along their sides, as the green of the land cover map shows.

The water layer of the map, OpenStreetMap's rivers, streams, canals, reservoirs and ponds, is incomplete for the springs and seasonal streams. Even so, 84 of the 450 fires with a point on the map and 32 of the 128 white phosphorus attacks lie within 500 m of mapped water.

What lives here

Between 2023 and 2024 Green Southerners recorded 245 sightings of 22 animals and 228 records of 11 trees and shrubs across the south. The most recorded tree is the Palestine oak (38 records), then the olive, the carob and the Aleppo oak; the most recorded animals are the golden jackal and the wild boar (23 each), the common kestrel and the red fox.

The attacks over the land

From 1 March 2026 the database records 451 fires and 128 white phosphorus attacks in the south, each kind collected and counted apart. Detonations and demolitions are not shown for now: their collection is not complete. The fires shown are every fire linked to an Israeli attack and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle.

Where the ground a fire burned was measured on Sentinel-2, it comes to 6,621.7 hectares over 238 fires. On the land cover map of 2025 that ground was 59.7% rangeland, 16.9% crops and 23.3% built area, which at 10 m takes in gardens and plots round the houses; less than 1% of it was land the map draws as trees.

70 of the 228 tree records and 94 of the 245 wildlife records lie within 1 km of a fire or a white phosphorus attack of the war: 17 of the 38 Palestine oaks, 9 of the 23 wild boar sightings, 7 of the 12 white storks and 5 of the 8 striped hyenas. That is a coincidence in space. It is not a finding that an attack harmed an animal or a tree, which these records cannot establish.

Limits
  • The wildlife and tree records are field records of 2023 and 2024, not a census: a place without one is a place not surveyed, and a species recorded once may live in many more places.
  • Lying within 1 km of an attack is a coincidence in space, not a finding of harm.
  • An attack known only by its village is measured from the village's point, so its distances are approximate.
  • The land cover is a model of 2025 from 10 m pictures, read here at about 27 m for the shares: the land before the war, not the damage.
  • The water layer maps only part of the springs and seasonal streams.
  • The photographs show the places and species named, at the dates given; the gazelle and the chameleon were photographed outside Lebanon.
Reading rules that apply to every figure on these pages
  • No figure is added across kinds of record. Each kind was looked for separately, over its own window and from its own sources, so a combined total would measure the collection rather than the war.
  • A record is not an event count. Records proven to describe the same event are folded together, but a record stays a unit of documentation.
  • Figures, not records. The maps and charts count records by town and week; a mark sits at its town and grows with the records there. The records themselves are not published: the database shows their figures. To access the records, contact Green Southerners.
  • Everything here is the 2026 war, from 1 March 2026. The wildlife and trees map, where it is shown, is earlier context recorded in 2023 and 2024, and says so.
  • Fires: every fire linked to an Israeli military attack (an attack documented at the same town on a day of the fire, or a source that names it), and every fire in the yellow-line zone or round Beaufort Castle, whatever lit it.
  • Absence is never published as a finding. An unshaded place carries no record of that kind, which is not evidence that nothing happened there.
Citing this page

Counts rise as more material is collected and can fall when duplicate records are folded together, so a citation needs the date of the snapshot it was read from.
